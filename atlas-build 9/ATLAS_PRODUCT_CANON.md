# 4PLANET ATLAS v3.0 — Product Canon

## What ATLAS Is

4PLANET ATLAS is a **spatial intelligence interface** for understanding how living systems connect to ecological challenges, viable solutions, and concrete action opportunities.

**Core function**: Help users explore where Earth's living systems are under pressure, who is working on solutions, where funding gaps remain, and how to participate in measurable restoration.

**Not just a globe**: ATLAS is an interpretive layer that converts scientific/ecological intelligence into geographic insight and actionable missions.

---

## What ATLAS Is NOT

❌ A real-time data platform (uses curated, reviewed data)  
❌ A satellite/monitoring system (shows analyzed intelligence, not raw feeds)  
❌ A political tool (neutral on geopolitics, focused on ecological systems)  
❌ A replacement for local expertise (amplifies and connects existing knowledge)  
❌ A finished product (a prototype communicating a vision)  

---

## Relationship to 4PLANET BRAIN

**BRAIN** = the data foundation.

- Stores sources (peer-reviewed, institutional, observational)
- Stores claims (species occurrence, threat status, ecosystem health, funding requirements)
- Tracks confidence (verified, sourced, curated, draft)
- Manages partners and collaboration status
- Owns financial logic (funding gaps, impact unit costs, ROI modeling)

**ATLAS** trusts and uses BRAIN data but does not modify it.

---

## Relationship to Living Systems Intelligence

**Living Systems Intelligence** = the explanatory layer.

Chains showing:
- Species / Ecosystem
- → Ecological Function
- → Ecosystem Service
- → Human System Impact
- → Threat
- → Solution Direction
- → 4PLANET Mission
- → Impact Unit

ATLAS visualizes these chains spatially.

**Example**:
```
Western Honey Bee
→ Pollination
→ Crop pollination
→ Food production + nutrition
→ Threat: pesticide use / monoculture
→ Solution: regenerative agriculture / protection zones
→ Mission: SPECIES
→ Impact: hectares of land under pollinator-friendly management
```

---

## Relationship to Mission Engine

**Mission Engine** = operational conversion.

Takes geographic + intelligence patterns and formalizes them into:
- Named missions (AM4ZONIA, CLIM4TE, SPECIES, WH4LES, COR4L, E4RTH)
- Defined problems
- Target living systems + geographies
- Success metrics
- Partner requirements
- Funding gap quantification

ATLAS shows where missions are active and where gaps remain.

---

## Relationship to Impact Made Easy

**Impact Made Easy** = participation layer.

Lets anyone contribute to missions via:
- Funding (specific impact units with cost transparency)
- Volunteering (partner coordination)
- Advocacy (data sharing, local collaboration)
- Reporting (verification of outcomes)

ATLAS helps you understand *why* your contribution matters by showing the causal chain.

---

## Relationship to 4PLANET OS

**4PLANET OS** = operational backbone.

Manages:
- Partner networks
- Funding flows + financial tracking
- Task/mission progress
- Member profiles + contributions
- Reporting + impact verification

ATLAS feeds OS with geographic insights.  
OS feeds ATLAS with operational status updates.

## Core User Journeys

### Journey 1: Scientist / Ecologist

```
1. Open ATLAS
2. Explore: "Where is biodiversity most threatened?"
3. Click node: Amazon Rainforest
4. See: threats, species, ecosystem services, loss rate
5. See: Living Systems Chain (forest → carbon → climate)
6. See: AM4ZONIA mission + partners
7. See: funding gap for protection
8. Act: Contribute to mission or collaborate with partners
```

### Journey 2: Funder / Philanthropist

```
1. Open ATLAS
2. Explore: "Where are the biggest gaps in funding?"
3. Filter: Funding gaps layer
4. Click: Coral Triangle
5. See: species at risk, ecosystem health, restoration cost
6. See: Solution direction + partner track record
7. See: Impact units (e.g. m² restored reef)
8. Invest: Fund specific mission or restoration partnership
```

### Journey 3: Policy Maker

```
1. Open ATLAS
2. Explore: "What living systems underpin our economy?"
3. Click: Local watershed / forest / fishery
4. See: Living Systems Chain showing services
5. See: Threats with quantified impacts
6. See: Existing solutions + partners
7. Decide: Policy alignment with existing efforts
```

### Journey 4: Activist / NGO

```
1. Open ATLAS
2. Explore: Global threats + solutions
3. Identify: Underserved geographic areas
4. See: Related missions + gaps
5. See: Potential partners + their status
6. Organize: Propose new mission or area focus
```

---

## What Must Remain Human-Reviewed

These should never be automated:

✅ Claim verification (must have human review)  
✅ Source quality assessment (human judgment required)  
✅ Partner credibility (on-ground knowledge required)  
✅ Ecological interpretation (complex, context-dependent)  
✅ Mission success metrics (require local expertise)  
✅ Funding gap estimation (requires updated ground truth)

**ATLAS is a tool for humans, not a replacement for human expertise.**

## Future Data Integrations

Planned sources (not yet active):

- **NASA** for Earth imagery + climate indicators
- **GBIF** for species occurrence mapping
- **IUCN Red List** for species threat status
- **Global Forest Watch** for forest loss monitoring
- **Protected Planet (WDPA)** for protected area coverage
- **Ocean Health Index** for ocean ecosystem health
- **Global Fishing Watch** for fishing pressure mapping
- **Natural Earth** for geographic reference
- **Project Drawdown** for climate solutions
- **Restor** for restoration outcomes tracking

**ATLAS should become an interpretation layer on trusted sources, not a data authority.**
